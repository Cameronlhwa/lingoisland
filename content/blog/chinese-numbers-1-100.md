If you want a clean, searchable reference for Chinese numbers 1 to 100 — here it is. For the conceptual overview (including 万 grouping and 两 vs 二), read [Mandarin Numbers: The Complete Guide](/blog/mandarin-numbers-complete-guide). For measure words in real sentences, read [Counting in Chinese](/blog/counting-in-chinese).

But first, one thing worth knowing: you don't need to memorise all 100 individually. Once you learn 1–10, the rest is pure pattern.

### The Pattern in One Sentence

Every number from 11 to 99 is built by combining the tens digit + 十 (shí) + the units digit.

- 11 = 十一 (shí yī)
- 35 = 三十五 (sān shí wǔ)
- 88 = 八十八 (bā shí bā)

No irregulars. No surprises. Just logic.

### Two Things to Remember

**Use 两 (liǎng) before measure words, not 二 (èr).** Ordering two coffees? That's 两杯咖啡 (liǎng bēi kāfēi), not 二杯咖啡.

**Mandarin uses 万 (wàn) for ten-thousands.** So 50,000 is 五万 (wǔ wàn), not 五十千. This trips up English speakers until it becomes habit.
